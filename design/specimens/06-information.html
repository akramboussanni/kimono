Information inside an app — sheet 06

Six ways to lay out what an app actually contains, using the household's own notes as the content. Each one borrows a Japanese object whose structure already encodes something: what is one record, what groups them, what ranks them, what orders them in time.

短冊Tanzaku — one record per strip

The paper strips hung on bamboo at Tanabata, one wish each. A record is a physical thing you can hang, take down, or cross out — which makes "one note" a unit rather than a row in a list.

和綴じWatoji — stitched, and the thread is the grouping

Japanese stab binding shows its stitches down the spine. Here the binding is the structure: a group is a run of pages held by one thread, so grouping is visible at the edge rather than announced by a heading colour.

重箱Jūbako — a tier per category

Stacked lacquer boxes, each tier holding one kind of thing. Counts sit in the tier they belong to, so the totals are never a separate dashboard from the contents.

番付Banzuke — rank shown as size

The sumo ranking sheet prints the top of the division largest and shrinks down the card, so standing is legible before a single number is read. Useful anywhere a count only matters relative to the others.

絵巻Emaki — history unrolled

The horizontal picture scroll reads left to right through time, one continuous surface rather than a stack of dated cards. Suits activity, deploys, and backups — anything where the gaps between events matter as much as the events.
